# Module 2 — X-11

X-11 (1965, US Census Bureau; refined into X-11-ARIMA and X-12/X-13) is **filter-based**: no
model, just a fixed sequence of moving averages applied iteratively. It is older than SEATS,
still the default in many agencies, and much easier to understand end to end. Doing it first
gives you a working mental picture of "what a seasonal adjustment *is*" before Module 4 makes
it model-based.

## The core loop, in one breath

Assume multiplicative $Z = T \times S \times I$ (take logs and it is additive).

1. **First trend estimate** — a centred 12-term moving average of $Z$ gives $\hat T$.
   (12 terms with half-weights on the ends: $\tfrac{1}{24}(1,2,2,\dots,2,1)$ — this is the
   filter that kills a 12-month seasonal exactly.)
2. **SI ratios** — $Z / \hat T$ leaves seasonal + irregular.
3. **Seasonal estimate** — for each calendar month separately, smooth its SI ratios across
   years with a **3×3 or 3×5 moving average**. Centre so the factors average to 1 over a year.
4. **Seasonally adjusted series** — $Z / \hat S$.
5. **Better trend** — apply a **Henderson filter** (9, 13 or 23 terms) to the adjusted series.
6. **Go to 2.** Twice through, roughly, with extreme-value replacement in between.

That is genuinely it. The complexity of real X-11 is all in the details of steps 3 and 5 and in
the outlier handling — not in the concept.

## The point of the module

Two things you should carry forward:

1. **X-11 is a linear filter** (ignoring the outlier steps). So it has a frequency response you
   can compute and plot. Once you can plot the X-11 gain function, X-11 and SEATS become
   comparable objects — both are filters — and Module 3 gives you the language for that
   comparison.
2. **End filters are different from interior filters**, and that difference is where every
   revision comes from. The Henderson end weights differ sharply from the interior weights;
   this is the mechanism behind adjustment failing *at* a turning point rather than before it.
   See [[10-14-forecasting]] and [[50-00-diagnostics-map]].
