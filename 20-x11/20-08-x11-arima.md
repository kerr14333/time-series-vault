# X-11-ARIMA — forecast extension

Code: `R/20-08-x11-arima.R`

The bridge back to Module 1, and the reason X-13 is called **X-13ARIMA-SEATS** rather than just X-13.

## Dagum's idea

Estimela Bee Dagum, Statistics Canada, 1980. If the trouble is that the symmetric filter has no future data to work with ([[20-07-end-filters]]), then **manufacture the future**: fit an ARIMA model, forecast one to three years ahead, backcast the same at the start, and apply the ordinary symmetric filters to the extended series.

That is the whole idea. Everything in Module 1 exists in X-13 to serve this one step.

## Why it beats a purely empirical end filter

Musgrave's filters minimise revision under an **assumed** model — a linear trend plus noise with a nominated I/C ratio, the same assumption for every series. Forecast extension uses a model **fitted to the series in front of you**, which knows its actual seasonal persistence ($\Theta$), its actual trend behaviour, its outliers and its calendar effects.

Dagum's results, and everything since, show materially smaller revisions. Typical practice: extend by **one year** of forecasts (X-13's default is one year of forecasts, and backcasts optionally).

## What the model contributes, concretely

Recall from [[10-10-airline-model]] that $\Theta$ measures how much the seasonal pattern is allowed to evolve. A high $\Theta$ means a stable seasonal, which means the forecast of next January is well pinned down by past Januaries, which means the extended-series end filter is close to the symmetric one, which means **small revisions**.

So the chain runs:

```
Theta (a fitted coefficient)  ->  forecast quality at the end
                              ->  how asymmetric the effective end filter is
                              ->  the size of the revisions you will publish
```

You can read the last link off the first number. That is the single most practically useful thing ARIMA gives a seasonal adjuster, and it is why Module 1 came first.

## And the limits

Forecast extension does not repeal [[20-07-end-filters]]; it improves the terms.

> [!warning] The forecast is still a forecast
> At a business-cycle turning point the ARIMA model extrapolates the **old** regime, because that is all the data it has seen. So the extension is wrong in a systematic direction exactly when it matters most, and the end-of-sample adjustment inherits that error.
>
> A better model shrinks the revision in normal times. It does not fix the turning point. Nothing does — the information genuinely is not there yet.

This is mechanism 1 of the three in [[20-07-end-filters]], and it is the subject of [[50-06-turning-points]].

## Lineage

| Version | Year | What it added |

The X-11 core has barely changed since 1965. What has been built around it is model-based: better ends, better preadjustment, and finally an entirely model-based alternative decomposition — which is Module 4.

## Exercises

1. Adjust a series with and without forecast extension (`x11.appendfcst`). Compare the last 12 months of D11.
2. Measure the improvement: revision from concurrent to final, both ways, averaged over the series.
3. Truncate the series just before a sharp turn, extend, and compare the forecast to the truth. This is mechanism 1 in isolation.
4. Relate revision size to the fitted $\Theta$ across several real series. Does the predicted relationship hold?
